# Contributing

This repository accepts papers on LLMs for political strategy, geopolitics, policymaking, strategic studies, and high-stakes decision-making.

Before proposing a paper, check `docs/selection-criteria.md` and `data/processed/thematic_papers.csv` to avoid duplicates.

## Required Fields

Please include:

- Title
- Year, or `n.d.` if unresolved
- Paper URL, preferably Semantic Scholar, DOI, arXiv, publisher, or ACL Anthology
- DOI or arXiv ID when available
- Proposed theme and subtheme
- Proposed label: `Core`, `Important`, `Curated`, or `Watchlist`
- Evidence type: `theory`, `survey`, `benchmark`, `dataset`, `empirical`, `method`, `position`, or `system`
- Citation count if available
- One short inclusion rationale
- One sentence explaining why it is not generic LLM, generic finance, generic business strategy, or generic safety work

## Quality Bar

Strong candidates usually satisfy at least one condition:

- Directly studies LLMs or LLM agents in political, policy, geopolitical, diplomatic, military, strategic, social-simulation, forecasting, or decision-making settings.
- Provides a benchmark, dataset, evaluation protocol, or empirical application for political or strategic LLM behavior.
- Is a field-shaping foundation in political science, international relations, strategic studies, intelligence analysis, or forecasting.
- Is a classical political NLP, text-as-data, or event-data paper that current LLM work builds on.

## Rejection Rules

Do not submit:

- Finance-only, trading, stock-prediction, portfolio-management, crypto, or generic financial LLM papers.
- Generic foundation-model papers without a direct politics, policy, geopolitics, strategy, or decision-making link.
- Generic safety papers unless the contribution is directly tied to political, institutional, influence, strategic, or national-security risk.
- Pure business or management-strategy papers unless the connection to public, political, institutional, or geopolitical decision-making is explicit.
- Blog posts, slides, or non-archival commentary unless they are canonical and no paper-like source exists.
- Pure benchmark papers whose tasks are not political, strategic, social, policy, diplomatic, military, or decision-making tasks.

Recent arXiv papers are allowed, but low-citation recent work should normally be labeled `Curated` or `Watchlist` until impact and relevance are clearer.
